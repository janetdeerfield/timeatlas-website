## The Conversion Baseline

Pacific Time (PST/PDT) operates exactly 3 hours behind Eastern Time (EST/EDT). If you are on the US East Coast and need to coordinate with the West Coast, subtract three hours from your current local time. If you are on the West Coast converting to Eastern, add three hours.

This offset is the foundational rule of US coast-to-coast scheduling. It applies to work meetings, broadcast times, customer service windows, live event countdowns, and any time-sensitive coordination between the Atlantic and Pacific coasts.

## Quick Reference: EST to PST

The following table outlines the most common conversion points across standard waking and working hours.

| Eastern Time (New York, Miami) | Pacific Time (Los Angeles, Seattle) |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| 9:00 AM EST | 6:00 AM PST |
| 12:00 PM EST (Noon) | 9:00 AM PST |
| 3:00 PM EST | 12:00 PM PST (Noon) |
| 5:00 PM EST | 2:00 PM PST |
| 8:00 PM EST | 5:00 PM PST |

For any time not listed, the rule is identical: subtract 3 hours. For the reverse conversion (PST to EST), add 3 hours.

## Coordinating Coast-to-Coast Meetings

When scheduling a synchronous meeting between the East and West coasts, standard 9-to-5 business hours do not perfectly overlap.

An early morning meeting at 9:00 AM EST lands at 6:00 AM PST — well before West Coast business hours begin. Conversely, a 4:00 PM PST meeting forces the East Coast team to stay online until 7:00 PM EST. Neither extreme is sustainable as a routine meeting time.

**The Optimal Window:** To ensure both parties are operating within standard business hours, schedule calls between **12:00 PM and 5:00 PM Eastern Time** (9:00 AM to 2:00 PM Pacific). This 5-hour corridor is the practical overlap zone for US coast-to-coast business.

## Travel and Flight Routing

When flying westbound from an EST airport — such as JFK or ATL — to a PST destination like LAX or SFO, travelers experience a 3-hour chronological shift.

A standard direct transcontinental flight takes approximately 6 hours of physical travel time. However, because you are crossing three time zones, you will only "lose" 3 hours on the clock. If you depart New York at 8:00 AM EST, you will land in Los Angeles at approximately 11:00 AM PST — despite 6 hours of airborne travel.

Indirect flights routed through central hubs such as DFW or ORD will extend your physical travel time, but the final 3-hour time zone delta remains absolute regardless of the routing.

## Daylight Saving Time: Does the Gap Change?

No. The 3-hour difference between Eastern and Pacific time is mathematically constant year-round.

Both time zones observe Daylight Saving Time simultaneously. On the second Sunday in March, both regions spring forward by one hour. On the first Sunday in November, both regions fall back by one hour. While the abbreviations shift — EST becomes EDT, and PST becomes PDT — the 3-hour offset between the two coasts never changes.

This synchronization is by design. Because the entire contiguous US shifts on the same calendar dates, no coast-to-coast scheduling built around the 3-hour rule requires any adjustment for DST.
